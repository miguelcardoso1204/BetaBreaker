\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

Indoor climbing gyms rely on frequent route rotation and community engagement to retain members, yet route information, beta knowledge, and progression tracking remain largely informal. This project presents Beta Breaker, a cross-platform mobile application that connects climbers and gyms through route discovery, ascent logging, beta video sharing, and gamification. Built with React Native and Expo on a Supabase backend, the system uses PostgreSQL Row Level Security as its authorization boundary, eliminating the need for a custom API server. The application supports QR-based scan-to-route flows. Features such as offline support, real-time competition scoreboards, video verification, style taxonomy, monetization, admin portal, and route-setting tools are planned for future phases. A strict Test-Driven Development methodology was followed throughout the implementation, producing 909 automated tests across the implemented phases. As of May 2026, 13 of 21 planned phases are fully implemented, with the remaining phases under development.

\vspace{1.5cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} climbing; gym; gamification; beta; mobile

\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Context}

The project consists of developing a mobile application to support the digitalization and gamification of indoor climbing gyms. The application will allow climbers to view information about each climbing route, such as grade, wall location, and skills emphasized (e.g., finger strength, technique, dynamic movement, footwork, endurance). Users will also be able to submit beta videos, leave comments, and review routes to help others progress.

To enhance motivation, the app will include gamification \cite{DeDi11} features such as achievements (badges for completing challenges, milestone ascents, or gym-specific objectives) and leaderboards (ranking climbers based on performance, number of routes completed, or difficulty level). Leaderboards will be complemented with prizes to reward top performers and stimulate continuous participation.

Additionally, the app will allow climbers to provide structured feedback to route setters, enabling gyms to collect insights about route quality, difficulty perception, and overall user experience.

The main goal is to create a community-driven platform that increases engagement with climbing gyms, motivates climbers through interactive features, and provides a structured way to analyze training and route styles.

Indoor climbers often lack a unified way to access up-to-date route information, beta videos, and meaningful progression tracking inside their gyms. Gyms also need a simple method to manage their routes and receive feedback from the climbers. Beta Breaker addresses these issues by providing climbers with clear route information, shared beta, achievements, and leaderboards that encourage consistent participation, while giving gyms a modern digital layer to manage their setting and community.

Beta Breaker is a platform that connects climbers and gyms around shared climbing experiences. It helps gyms publish and manage their indoor routes and community, while giving climbers a simple way to discover, log, and learn from their climbs. The product emphasizes fairness, meaningful feedback, and clear insight into progression, supporting both everyday sessions and gym-led initiatives such as seasons and events.

\textbf{Product name:} Beta Breaker

\paragraph{Vision statement.} Create the go-to mobile platform that connects climbers and gyms through route discovery, beta sharing, and light-weight gamification. Beta Breaker motivates climbers to progress, helps gyms understand their community, and gives route setters actionable feedback to improve route quality.

\paragraph{Solution.} A mobile app (iOS/Android) where climbers can browse routes, watch/upload beta videos, leave comments and ratings, and participate in challenges, achievements, and leaderboards. Gyms and route setters receive summarized feedback and simple analytics on route reception and difficulty perception.

\paragraph{Primary users.} (1)~Climbers who want to progress and engage with the community; (2)~Route setters / gyms who want structured feedback and engagement tools.

\section{Motivation}

Indoor climbing is highly social, but knowledge about routes (beta, perceived difficulty, style) is fragmented across word-of-mouth and social media. Gyms lack structured, privacy-respectful ways to engage customers and collect feedback; climbers lack a single place to log, learn, and stay motivated.

The problem can be decomposed into the following sub-problems:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Identity \& Accounts:} registration, profile, privacy and safety.
    \item \textbf{Gyms \& Areas:} gym directory, sectors, social links.
    \item \textbf{Routes Catalog:} route model, QR/NFC IDs, user-driven tags and feedback.
    \item \textbf{Logging \& Sessions:} quick logs, session start/end, summaries.
    \item \textbf{Analytics:} grade conversions, personalized suggestions (Pro).
    \item \textbf{Gamification:} badges, streaks, optional rank model.
    \item \textbf{Social:} leaderboards, feedback, reporting, video verification policy.
    \item \textbf{Admin Web:} audit logs and operations.
    \item \textbf{Monetization:} Pro subscriptions and gym billing console.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Increase climber engagement and retention through scan-to-log flows and rich feedback.
    \item Provide gyms with verifiable leaderboards, operational visibility, and simple billing.
    \item Enable fair progression by crowd-sourced tags and optional video verification.
    \item Monetize via Pro subscriptions (consumers) and per-active-user billing (gyms).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Project Scope}

\paragraph{In Scope (MVP).}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Route catalog:} Browse/search routes by grade, wall/sector, style/tags (e.g., finger strength, dynamic movement, footwork, endurance).
    \item \textbf{Beta and reviews:} View, upload, and report beta videos (basic moderation tools); leave comments and 1--5 rating plus perceived grade.
    \item \textbf{Gamification:} Achievements (e.g., first of grade, weekly streaks), simple leaderboards (per gym, per period), and optional prizes managed by gyms.
    \item \textbf{Feedback to setters:} Structured form per route (quality, grade perception, style markers); aggregated summaries and trends for gyms.
    \item \textbf{Accounts \& privacy:} Email/social login, profile, basic notification settings, GDPR-aligned consent screens.
    \item \textbf{Admin (lightweight):} Gym/route management and review moderation (web or mobile admin views).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Out of Scope.} Advanced training plans, wearables integration, Elo-like rank systems, and outdoor crag support.

\chapter{Relevance and Feasibility}

\section{Relevance}

Indoor climbing gyms continue to grow and increasingly rely on community engagement and fresh route setting to retain members. However, route information, style perception, and beta knowledge are still largely informal and fragmented. A platform that is tightly connected to the physical gym environment and its route lifecycle can improve the experience for climbers while providing gyms with structured feedback and actionable insights.

\section{Feasibility}

The project is designed as an MVP-first solution with an incremental delivery strategy. The scope is controlled to avoid complex payment flows and heavy analytics at this stage, focusing instead on core user value: route discovery, logging, feedback, and lightweight gamification. Technical feasibility is supported by a standard client--server architecture and commonly used technologies appropriate for iterative development.

\subsection{SWOT Analysis}

\paragraph{Strengths}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Clear niche focus (indoor climbing) with community-driven content.
    \item Gamification aligned with climbers' intrinsic motivation and progression.
    \item Actionable, structured feedback loop for route setters and gyms.
    \item Lightweight tech stack suitable for a student project with iterative delivery.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Weaknesses}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Reliance on user-generated content (requires seeding and moderation).
    \item Limited resources for content review, partnerships, and support.
    \item Potential data sparsity if a gym's community adopts slowly (network effects).
    \item Early analytics are basic; may not meet advanced gym expectations.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Opportunities}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Rapid growth of indoor climbing and new gyms seeking differentiation.
    \item Partnerships with gyms for events, challenges, and prize sponsorships.
    \item Extensions to training insights (style profiling, personalized goals).
    \item Integrations with existing gym systems (check-in, route databases) and social platforms.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Threats}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Competing apps or gyms' own solutions reducing adoption incentives.
    \item Content/privacy incidents (e.g., inappropriate uploads) harming trust.
    \item Platform policy changes (app stores) affecting distribution or features.
    \item Low engagement if gamification is mis-tuned or prizes are inconsistent.
\end{itemize}

\section{Comparative Analysis with Existing Solutions}

\subsection{Existing Solutions}

A preliminary market scan shows tools that address only parts of the problem: (i)~generic training/logbook applications, usually centered on the user and not tied to a gym's live route set; (ii)~internal or informal gym route management approaches (e.g., boards/spreadsheets) that lack structured community feedback; and (iii)~social platforms that enable sharing, but without a direct connection to routes, setters, seasons, and aggregated gym analytics.

Overall, no direct competitor was identified that offers an end-to-end product combining: gym route management, in-gym route discovery, ascent logging, and structured feedback. Beta Breaker further plans to add optional video verification for fair leaderboards and aggregated style/statistics at both route and gym level.

\section{Innovation Proposal and Added Value}

The main innovation lies in merging the climber experience and gym operations into one connected platform. Research suggests that gamification can positively influence engagement and motivation in digital services \cite{HaKo14}. Each ascent can generate value for both sides: climbers receive a richer, more motivating experience (history, progress, community beta, and gamification), while gyms and setters receive structured feedback, difficulty perception signals, and usage trends.

Key added-value points include: (i)~an in-gym, scan-to-route flow that reduces friction; (ii)~a planned controlled style taxonomy and crowd-based style characterization that can be aggregated into route and gym profiles; (iii)~lightweight gamification (badges, seasons, leaderboards, prizes) to improve retention; and (iv)~a data model built to support aggregated statistics without constant expensive recomputation.

\section{Business Opportunity}

The business opportunity is primarily supported by the lack of direct competition identified for a gym-oriented, integrated solution. The product naturally fits a B2B2C positioning: gyms adopt the platform to strengthen community engagement and improve route lifecycle visibility, while climbers gain a centralized and motivating experience.

A plausible monetization path is subscription/licensing per gym (tiered by feature modules or active users), optionally complemented by premium consumer features. Market entry can start with a small number of pilot gyms to validate adoption, refine the user experience, and then expand to other gyms with similar needs.

\section{Business Risks}

The following risks could affect delivery or adoption of the MVP. Each item includes a primary mitigation.

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Cold start (adoption/content).} Few early users or videos reduce value. \emph{Mitigation:} seed routes and beta with a pilot gym; launch a short challenge to generate initial content.

    \item \textbf{Content safety.} Inappropriate or IP-infringing uploads harm trust. \emph{Mitigation:} clear guidelines, report/takedown flow, uploader ownership affirmation.

    \item \textbf{Video performance/cost.} Upload/streaming can be slow or exceed free tiers. \emph{Mitigation:} client-side compression, duration/resolution caps, lazy loading, usage monitoring.

    \item \textbf{Scope creep and schedule slip.} Extra features delay MVP. \emph{Mitigation:} milestone feature freeze, time-boxed sprints, ``later'' backlog.

    \item \textbf{Security \& privacy.} Weak auth or poor consent risks data exposure/GDPR issues. \emph{Mitigation:} managed auth, HTTPS, rate limits, least-privilege, consent + delete/export.

    \item \textbf{Third-party dependence.} Changes in auth/storage/CDN pricing or APIs. \emph{Mitigation:} pin versions, thin integration layer, periodic review of usage and costs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Constraints}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]

\subsection{Non-Functional Requirements}

The following nonfunctional requirements define the expected quality attributes of the system.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2cm]

    \item \textbf{NFR-1 -- Performance and Responsiveness.}
    The mobile app shall load the Home screen and route details within 3 seconds on a stable 4G or Wi-Fi connection. Core actions (scanning a QR, logging a send) must provide visible feedback within 1 second.

    \item \textbf{NFR-2 -- Availability.}
    Cloud services (API and database) shall maintain at least 99\% uptime per month, excluding scheduled maintenance.

    \item \textbf{NFR-3 -- Offline Operation.}
    Climbers shall be able to view cached routes and log attempts while offline. The app shall automatically sync new data when a connection is restored.

    \item \textbf{NFR-4 -- Data Integrity and Security.}
    All communication between client and server must use HTTPS (TLS 1.2+). User credentials are encrypted and never stored in plain text. Access control is enforced according to user roles (FR-L1).

    \item \textbf{NFR-5 -- Privacy and Data Protection.}
    The system shall comply with GDPR requirements, allowing users to export or delete their data (FR-A3). Sensitive media such as verification videos must respect privacy settings and consent flags.

    \item \textbf{NFR-6 -- Scalability.}
    The backend shall support up to 10\,000 concurrent users without degraded performance, allowing horizontal scaling through containerized deployment.

    \item \textbf{NFR-7 -- Maintainability.}
    The codebase shall follow modular architecture, version control via Git, and continuous integration to enable safe incremental updates.

    \item \textbf{NFR-8 -- Usability and Accessibility.}
    The interface shall follow consistent design guidelines with clear iconography and large tap targets (FR-Q2). The app must remain fully usable on common mobile screens and include a high-contrast or color-aware mode (FR-Q3).

    \item \textbf{NFR-9 -- Reliability and Recovery.}
    In case of service interruption, locally stored session logs must not be lost. Sync operations shall retry automatically until confirmation of success.

    \item \textbf{NFR-10 -- Monitoring and Logging.}
    The backend shall collect anonymized error and performance metrics for issue diagnosis, while excluding personal identifiers.

    \item \textbf{NFR-11 -- Video and Media.}
    Beta videos shall have a maximum duration of 60 seconds and a maximum resolution of 1080p. Uploaded files must not exceed 150\,MB after client-side compression. Videos are hosted on Cloudinary via unsigned upload presets. Lists use lazy-loaded thumbnails; playback uses adaptive streaming.

    \item \textbf{NFR-12 -- Rate Limiting.}
    API endpoints and Edge Functions shall enforce rate limits to prevent abuse (e.g., max 60 requests per minute per authenticated user). Repeated violations result in temporary lockout.

    \item \textbf{NFR-13 -- Error Recovery.}
    The application shall display user-friendly error messages and provide retry options for transient failures. Critical errors shall be captured by the error tracking service with PII scrubbed.

    \item \textbf{NFR-14 -- Continuous Integration.}
    Every push to the main branch shall trigger an automated pipeline: lint $\rightarrow$ type-check $\rightarrow$ test suite. Merges are blocked if any stage fails.

\end{itemize}

\section{Modeling}

\subsection{Data Model (ER)}

The data model was designed to support both the climber perspective (ascent logging, feedback, and media) and the gym perspective (route management, seasons/leaderboards, and aggregated analytics). At its core, \textit{users}, \textit{gyms}, and \textit{routes} form the main domain entities. Each route belongs to a gym and stores relevant metadata (grade system/value, color, wall section, setter, and lifecycle status).

Ascent logging is represented by \textit{route\_ascents}, which associates a user to a route and stores information such as whether the route was sent, the date, rating, and optional comments. Style characterization is supported by a controlled taxonomy (\textit{style\_tags}). Users can select tags per ascent using the association table \textit{ascent\_style\_tags}. These raw votes can then be aggregated into \textit{route\_style\_statistics} (per-route) and \textit{gym\_style\_statistics} (per-gym), enabling trend and profile analysis without recomputing everything in real time.

Motivation and community features are supported by \textit{badges}/\textit{user\_badges} and by \textit{leaderboards} with entries and participation snapshots. Optional \textit{prizes} can be linked to leaderboard rankings to encourage consistent participation.

\chapter{Developed Solution}

\section{Introduction}

The Beta Breaker application has been implemented as a cross-platform mobile application using React Native \cite{RNDo25} and Expo \cite{ExDo25}, backed entirely by Supabase \cite{SuDo25} for database, authentication, storage, and real-time capabilities. As of May 2026, the project has completed 13 of 21 planned phases, covering the core platform features: authentication, route catalog, session logging, QR scanning, gamification, social features, notifications, beta video sharing, progression analytics, profile management, onboarding, internationalization (EN + PT-PT), accessibility, and Sentry error monitoring. Features such as offline support, competitions/events, video verification, style taxonomy, monetization, route-setting tools, and admin portal are planned for future phases.

The implementation follows a strict Test-Driven Development (TDD) \cite{Beck03} methodology, resulting in 909 automated tests across the implemented phases.

\section{Architecture}

The system is organized into two main tiers: a React Native \cite{RNDo25} client built with Expo \cite{ExDo25}, and a Supabase \cite{SuDo25} backend that provides the full server-side infrastructure --- PostgreSQL database, authentication (GoTrue), object storage, real-time change streams, and serverless Edge Functions.

This architecture pushes authorization down to the database level \cite{SiKo19}: every table is protected by Row Level Security (RLS) \cite{PgDo25} policies that enforce access control based on the authenticated user's identity and role. Because RLS is evaluated by PostgreSQL itself, the security boundary cannot be bypassed by client-side code or misconfigured API routes --- a stronger guarantee than middleware-based authorization in a traditional API server. The client communicates with the database through PostgREST (auto-generated REST endpoints) and with auxiliary services through five Deno-based Edge Functions: QR code signing, push notification dispatch, in-app purchase verification, billing webhooks, and promo code redemption.

State management is organized into three layers:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Server state (TanStack Query v5 \cite{TaDo25}):} All data from Supabase (routes, ascents, leaderboards, badges). The database is the source of truth; the client caches and auto-refetches.
    \item \textbf{Client state (Zustand 5.x \cite{ZuDo25}):} Ephemeral UI state such as session timer, pending logs, active filters, and bottom sheet visibility. Minimal boilerplate, no providers required.
    \item \textbf{Local persistence (expo-sqlite, planned):} Offline route cache with 24-hour TTL and an offline action queue. On reconnect, actions will be replayed with exponential backoff (1s, 4s, 16s).
\end{enumerate}

The data flow follows four patterns:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Reads:} Screen $\rightarrow$ TanStack Query hook $\rightarrow$ service layer $\rightarrow$ PostgREST $\rightarrow$ Postgres (RLS-filtered).
    \item \textbf{Writes:} User action $\rightarrow$ Zustand optimistic update $\rightarrow$ useMutation $\rightarrow$ service $\rightarrow$ Postgres triggers (streak/badge/leaderboard) $\rightarrow$ cache invalidation.
    \item \textbf{Offline (planned):} Action $\rightarrow$ offlineStore queue (SQLite) $\rightarrow$ [reconnect] $\rightarrow$ drain queue $\rightarrow$ service layer $\rightarrow$ invalidate caches.
    \item \textbf{Realtime:} Supabase Realtime subscription $\rightarrow$ Postgres emits changes $\rightarrow$ TanStack Query cache updated.
\end{itemize}

Services are thin wrappers around Supabase PostgREST. They contain no business logic, only query construction:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \texttt{auth.service.ts} --- Sign-up, sign-in, sign-out, password reset, session management.
    \item \texttt{routes.service.ts} --- Route queries with grade, style, and status filters.
    \item \texttt{sessions.service.ts} --- Ascent logging, session summaries, session history.
    \item \texttt{leaderboard.service.ts} --- Three scoring models (hardest grade, flash rate, volume).
    \item \texttt{media.service.ts} --- Beta video upload to Cloudinary (unsigned preset), metadata storage, retrieval, deletion, and like/unlike mutations.
    \item \texttt{purchase.service.ts} --- Wraps \texttt{react-native-iap} \cite{IaDo25} for IAP purchase and receipt verification.
    \item Plus 20 additional services for gyms, profiles, feedback, social, events, scores, challenges, badges, notifications, moderation, saved routes, subscriptions, billing, trials, dashboard, audit, maintenance, consensus, calendar, and seasons.
\end{itemize}

    \item \texttt{useSettingCalendar} --- Route setting session scheduling and workload.

    \item Plus 30 additional hooks for gyms, sessions, saved routes, feedback, media, social, badges, leaderboards, notifications, events, scores, challenges, admin routes, audit log, maintenance tickets, moderation, grade pyramid, style insights, route suggestions, seasons, billing, entitlements, export results, trials, and gym leaderboards.

\section{Validation Strategy}

The testing and validation plan for Beta Breaker is designed to demonstrate that the solution fulfils the functional and non-functional requirements defined in Chapter~3, operates correctly under realistic conditions, and is ready for pilot deployment with real users and gyms.

The strategy combines three complementary approaches:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Automated regression testing} --- Every feature is developed using Test-Driven Development (TDD): failing tests are written before the implementation, ensuring that every requirement has verifiable acceptance criteria from inception. This guards against regressions as the codebase grows.
    \item \textbf{Security validation via integration tests} --- Because authorization is enforced at the database level through Row Level Security (RLS) policies, correctness cannot be verified through unit tests alone. Integration tests authenticate as different user roles against a real PostgreSQL instance to confirm that data isolation holds.
    \item \textbf{User acceptance and operational validation (Projeto~II)} --- End-to-end device tests and structured user feedback sessions with real climbers and gym staff will validate usability, performance, and fitness for purpose in a production-like environment.
\end{enumerate}

This layered approach was chosen because the project's architecture pushes critical business rules into two distinct boundaries --- the database (RLS, triggers, stored functions) and the client (UI flows, offline queue, state management) --- each requiring a different testing technique.

\section{Test Levels}

\subsection{Unit Tests}

Unit tests validate individual modules in isolation:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Utility functions} --- Grade conversion (FR-P1), streak calculation (FR-F2), leaderboard scoring (FR-G1), Zod validation schemas (FR-D1), and entitlement checks (FR-L3). These are pure functions with no external dependencies.
    \item \textbf{Service layer} --- Each service module is tested with the Supabase client mocked, verifying that the correct query builder methods are called with the expected parameters.
    \item \textbf{Hook layer} --- TanStack Query hooks are tested with services mocked, verifying caching behavior, optimistic updates, error handling, and cache invalidation after mutations.
    \item \textbf{UI components} --- Rendered with React Native Testing Library, asserting on visible text, user interactions (press, scroll), and conditional rendering based on props and state.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Integration Tests}

Integration tests run against a real local Supabase instance (\texttt{supabase start}) with Docker containers for PostgreSQL, Auth (GoTrue), and Storage. Each test:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Creates users via the Auth API and obtains JWT tokens.
    \item Authenticates as a specific role (climber, gym admin, setter, super-admin).
    \item Performs operations (SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE) and asserts that RLS policies correctly allow or deny access.
\end{enumerate}

These tests cover all 126+ RLS policies across 30+ tables, database triggers (badge awarding, streak updates, leaderboard recomputation), stored functions (grade consensus, profile RPCs), CASCADE delete propagation, and UNIQUE constraint enforcement.

\subsection{End-to-End Tests (Planned --- Phase~20)}

Full device-level tests using Maestro or Detox will validate critical user flows: authentication, scan-to-log, ascent logging with offline sync, leaderboard updates, badge awarding, Pro subscription purchase, and admin route management. These are scheduled for Projeto~II.

\section{Development Methodology}

The project follows an incremental, test-driven approach \cite{Beck03, Somm15} organized into 21 sequential phases. Each phase is broken into numbered steps, and each step follows the TDD cycle:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Red:} Write failing tests that define the expected behavior.
    \item \textbf{Green:} Write the minimum code to make the tests pass.
    \item \textbf{Refactor:} Clean up duplication, improve naming, re-run all tests.
\end{enumerate}

Version control follows a trunk-based workflow on the \texttt{main} branch, with atomic commits per step. Each commit message references the step number (e.g., ``feat: add QR scanner screen (Step 7.1)''). A GitHub Actions CI pipeline runs lint, type-check, and the full test suite on every push.

\chapter{Conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}

This report documents the specification and implementation of Beta Breaker, a mobile platform for indoor climbing gyms. The project addressed the identified problem --- fragmented route knowledge and lack of structured gym-climber feedback --- by building a comprehensive system that connects climbers and gyms through route discovery, ascent logging, beta sharing, and gamification.

The implementation followed a strict TDD methodology across 13 completed phases, producing 909 automated tests. The system covers the core platform features: authentication, route catalog, session logging, QR scanning, gamification (19 badges, streaks, challenges), social features (leaderboards, follows, feedback, moderation), push notifications, beta video sharing, progression analytics (grade pyramid, personalized suggestions), profile management, onboarding, internationalization (EN + PT-PT), accessibility (color-aware mode, screen reader support), and Sentry error monitoring. Offline support, competitions, video verification, style taxonomy, monetization (Pro subscriptions, gym billing), route-setting tools, and the admin portal remain planned for future phases.

The architecture --- Supabase-first with no custom backend, TanStack Query for server state, Zustand for client state, and expo-sqlite for offline persistence --- proved effective for a single-developer project, minimizing infrastructure complexity while maintaining security through database-level RLS policies.

\section{Future Work}

The remaining work includes the following phases:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Phase 6 --- Offline Support:} SQLite action queue, offline route cache with 24-hour TTL, and sync engine with exponential backoff.
    \item \textbf{Phase 11 --- Competitions \& Events:} Event CRUD, score entry with judge workflow, live scoreboard via Supabase Realtime, and results export.
    \item \textbf{Phase 13 --- Monetization:} Pro subscription IAP flow, entitlements, paywall, trials, promo code redemption, and gym billing console.
    \item \textbf{Phase 15 --- Admin Portal \& Route-Setting:} Admin dashboard, route management, moderation queue, setting calendar, grade consensus, maintenance tickets, season management, and audit log.
    \item \textbf{Video verification and style taxonomy:} Video-based send verification for fair leaderboards, and crowd-sourced style characterization with aggregated insights.
    \item \textbf{Phase 20 --- End-to-End Testing \& Hardening:} E2E test suite (Maestro or Detox) covering critical user flows. Performance profiling with Sentry traces, rate limit configuration, load testing with scaled seed data, and security audit of RLS policies.
    \item \textbf{Phase 21 --- Build \& Deployment:} EAS Build profiles (development, preview, production), CI/CD pipeline finalization (lint $\rightarrow$ type-check $\rightarrow$ test $\rightarrow$ deploy), production Supabase project setup (migrations, storage buckets, Realtime, Edge Function secrets), and store submission.
\end{enumerate}

Additionally, one low-priority stretch goal remains: Step~8.5 (Elo-like rank system with decay rules, FR-F4, PW=1).

Completing these phases will result in a production-ready platform deployable to the App Store and Google Play, ready for pilot testing with partner climbing gyms.
