\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

Indoor climbing gyms rely on frequent route rotation and community engagement to retain members, yet route information, beta knowledge, and progression tracking remain largely informal. This project presents Beta Breaker, a cross-platform mobile application that connects climbers and gyms through route discovery, ascent logging, beta video sharing, and gamification. Built with React Native and Expo on a Supabase backend, the system uses PostgreSQL Row Level Security as its authorization boundary, eliminating the need for a custom API server. The application supports QR-based scan-to-route flows, offline operation via an SQLite action queue, real-time competition scoreboards, and a Pro subscription tier managed through native in-app purchases. A strict Test-Driven Development methodology was followed throughout the implementation, producing 1\,440 automated tests (1\,032 unit and 408 integration) across 69 completed development steps. As of February 2026, 12 of 21 planned phases are fully implemented, covering all ``Must Have'' requirements and providing a functional MVP ready for pilot deployment with partner gyms.

\vspace{1.5cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} climbing; gym; gamification; beta; mobile

\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Project Overview}

The project consists of developing a mobile application to support the digitalization and gamification of indoor climbing gyms. The application will allow climbers to view information about each climbing route, such as grade, wall location, and skills emphasized (e.g., finger strength, technique, dynamic movement, footwork, endurance). Users will also be able to submit beta videos, leave comments, and review routes to help others progress.

To enhance motivation, the app will include gamification features such as achievements (badges for completing challenges, milestone ascents, or gym-specific objectives) and leaderboards (ranking climbers based on performance, number of routes completed, or difficulty level). Leaderboards will be complemented with prizes to reward top performers and stimulate continuous participation.

Additionally, the app will allow climbers to provide structured feedback to route setters, enabling gyms to collect insights about route quality, difficulty perception, and overall user experience.

The main goal is to create a community-driven platform that increases engagement with climbing gyms, motivates climbers through interactive features, and provides a structured way to analyze training and route styles.

\section{Problem Statement}

Indoor climbers often lack a unified way to access up-to-date route information, beta videos, and meaningful progression tracking inside their gyms. Gyms also need a simple method to manage their routes and receive feedback from the climbers. Beta Breaker addresses these issues by providing climbers with clear route information, shared beta, achievements, and leaderboards that encourage consistent participation, while giving gyms a modern digital layer to manage their setting and community.

\section{High-Level Narrative}

Beta Breaker is a platform that connects climbers and gyms around shared climbing experiences. It helps gyms publish and manage their indoor routes and community, while giving climbers a simple way to discover, log, and learn from their climbs. The product emphasizes fairness, meaningful feedback, and clear insight into progression, supporting both everyday sessions and gym-led initiatives such as seasons and events.

\section{Vision}

\textbf{Product name:} Beta Breaker

\paragraph{Vision statement.} Create the go-to mobile platform that connects climbers and gyms through route discovery, beta sharing, and light-weight gamification. Beta Breaker motivates climbers to progress, helps gyms understand their community, and gives route setters actionable feedback to improve route quality.

\paragraph{Problem.} Indoor climbing is highly social, but knowledge about routes (beta, perceived difficulty, style) is fragmented across word-of-mouth and social media. Gyms lack structured, privacy-respectful ways to engage customers and collect feedback; climbers lack a single place to log, learn, and stay motivated.

\paragraph{Solution.} A mobile app (iOS/Android) where climbers can browse routes, watch/upload beta videos, leave comments and ratings, and participate in challenges, achievements, and leaderboards. Gyms and route setters receive summarized feedback and simple analytics on route reception and difficulty perception.

\paragraph{Primary users.} (1)~Climbers who want to progress and engage with the community; (2)~Route setters / gyms who want structured feedback and engagement tools.

\section{Objectives}

\subsection*{Business Objectives (Project Charter)}

The business objectives of this project are:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Support climbing gyms in offering a modern digital service that complements their physical routes.
    \item Increase customer engagement and retention by gamifying the climbing experience.
    \item Provide climbers with tools to track progress, exchange knowledge, and connect with the community.
    \item Motivate climbers through gamification features such as achievements, challenges, leaderboards, and prizes.
    \item Strengthen the visibility of climbing gyms through digital integration and user-generated content.
    \item Provide route setters with valuable feedback from climbers, helping them adjust and improve future routes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Business Goals (SRS)}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Increase climber engagement and retention through scan-to-log flows and rich feedback.
    \item Provide gyms with verifiable leaderboards, operational visibility, and simple billing.
    \item Enable fair progression by crowd-sourced tags and optional video verification.
    \item Monetize via Pro subscriptions (consumers) and per-active-user billing (gyms).
\end{itemize}

\section{Project Scope}

\subsection{In Scope (MVP)}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Route catalog:} Browse/search routes by grade, wall/sector, style/tags (e.g., finger strength, dynamic movement, footwork, endurance).
    \item \textbf{Beta and reviews:} View, upload, and report beta videos (basic moderation tools); leave comments and 1--5 rating plus perceived grade.
    \item \textbf{Gamification:} Achievements (e.g., first of grade, weekly streaks), simple leaderboards (per gym, per period), and optional prizes managed by gyms.
    \item \textbf{Feedback to setters:} Structured form per route (quality, grade perception, style markers); aggregated summaries and trends for gyms.
    \item \textbf{Accounts \& privacy:} Email/social login, profile, basic notification settings, GDPR-aligned consent screens.
    \item \textbf{Admin (lightweight):} Gym/route management and review moderation (web or mobile admin views).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Out of Scope (for now)}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Payments, in-app purchases, and complex prize logistics.
    \item Advanced training plans, wearables integration, or full performance analytics.
    \item Outdoor crag support.
\end{itemize}

    \item Pilot deployment with seeded routes for at least one gym and a small user group.
    \item Documentation (requirements, architecture, test plan, project report) and a final presentation.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]

    \item \textbf{Performance:} Route list loads in $< 2$\,s on average Wi-Fi; videos stream with adaptive quality.
    \item \textbf{Security \& privacy:} Least-privilege data access; user control over content and account data.
    \item \textbf{Maintainability:} Modular architecture; documented APIs and data models.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Relevance and Feasibility}

\section{Relevance}

Indoor climbing gyms continue to grow and increasingly rely on community engagement and fresh route setting to retain members. However, route information, style perception, and beta knowledge are still largely informal and fragmented. A platform that is tightly connected to the physical gym environment and its route lifecycle can improve the experience for climbers while providing gyms with structured feedback and actionable insights.

\section{Feasibility}

The project is designed as an MVP-first solution with an incremental delivery strategy. The scope is controlled to avoid complex payment flows and heavy analytics at this stage, focusing instead on core user value: route discovery, logging, feedback, and lightweight gamification. Technical feasibility is supported by a standard client--server architecture and commonly used technologies appropriate for iterative development.

\subsection{SWOT Analysis}

\paragraph{Strengths}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Clear niche focus (indoor climbing) with community-driven content.
    \item Gamification aligned with climbers' intrinsic motivation and progression.
    \item Actionable, structured feedback loop for route setters and gyms.
    \item Lightweight tech stack suitable for a student project with iterative delivery.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Weaknesses}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Reliance on user-generated content (requires seeding and moderation).
    \item Limited resources for content review, partnerships, and support.
    \item Potential data sparsity if a gym's community adopts slowly (network effects).
    \item Early analytics are basic; may not meet advanced gym expectations.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Opportunities}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Rapid growth of indoor climbing and new gyms seeking differentiation.
    \item Partnerships with gyms for events, challenges, and prize sponsorships.
    \item Extensions to training insights (style profiling, personalized goals).
    \item Integrations with existing gym systems (check-in, route databases) and social platforms.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Threats}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Competing apps or gyms' own solutions reducing adoption incentives.
    \item Content/privacy incidents (e.g., inappropriate uploads) harming trust.
    \item Platform policy changes (app stores) affecting distribution or features.
    \item Low engagement if gamification is mis-tuned or prizes are inconsistent.
\end{itemize}

\section{Comparative Analysis with Existing Solutions}

\subsection{Existing Solutions}

A preliminary market scan shows tools that address only parts of the problem: (i)~generic training/logbook applications, usually centered on the user and not tied to a gym's live route set; (ii)~internal or informal gym route management approaches (e.g., boards/spreadsheets) that lack structured community feedback; and (iii)~social platforms that enable sharing, but without a direct connection to routes, setters, seasons, and aggregated gym analytics.

Overall, no direct competitor was identified that offers an end-to-end product combining: gym route management, in-gym route discovery, ascent logging, structured feedback, optional video verification for fair leaderboards, and aggregated style/statistics at both route and gym level.

\section{Innovation Proposal and Added Value}

The main innovation lies in merging the climber experience and gym operations into one connected platform. Each ascent can generate value for both sides: climbers receive a richer, more motivating experience (history, progress, community beta, and gamification), while gyms and setters receive structured feedback, difficulty perception signals, and usage trends.

Key added-value points include: (i)~an in-gym, scan-to-route flow that reduces friction; (ii)~a controlled style taxonomy and crowd-based style characterization that can be aggregated into route and gym profiles; (iii)~lightweight gamification (badges, seasons, leaderboards, prizes) to improve retention; and (iv)~a data model built to support aggregated statistics without constant expensive recomputation.

\section{Business Opportunity}

The business opportunity is primarily supported by the lack of direct competition identified for a gym-oriented, integrated solution. The product naturally fits a B2B2C positioning: gyms adopt the platform to strengthen community engagement and improve route lifecycle visibility, while climbers gain a centralized and motivating experience.

A plausible monetization path is subscription/licensing per gym (tiered by feature modules or active users), optionally complemented by premium consumer features. Market entry can start with a small number of pilot gyms to validate adoption, refine the user experience, and then expand to other gyms with similar needs.

\section{Business Risks}

The following risks could affect delivery or adoption of the MVP. Each item includes a primary mitigation.

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Cold start (adoption/content).} Few early users or videos reduce value. \emph{Mitigation:} seed routes and beta with a pilot gym; launch a short challenge to generate initial content.

    \item \textbf{Content safety.} Inappropriate or IP-infringing uploads harm trust. \emph{Mitigation:} clear guidelines, report/takedown flow, uploader ownership affirmation.

    \item \textbf{Video performance/cost.} Upload/streaming can be slow or exceed free tiers. \emph{Mitigation:} client-side compression, duration/resolution caps, lazy loading, usage monitoring.

    \item \textbf{Scope creep and schedule slip.} Extra features delay MVP. \emph{Mitigation:} milestone feature freeze, time-boxed sprints, ``later'' backlog.

    \item \textbf{Security \& privacy.} Weak auth or poor consent risks data exposure/GDPR issues. \emph{Mitigation:} managed auth, HTTPS, rate limits, least-privilege, consent + delete/export.

    \item \textbf{Third-party dependence.} Changes in auth/storage/CDN pricing or APIs. \emph{Mitigation:} pin versions, thin integration layer, periodic review of usage and costs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Constraints}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Timeline.} Delivery within the Oct 2025--Jun 2026 academic schedule and stated milestones.
    \item \textbf{Budget.} No dedicated funding; rely on free/low-cost tiers and university resources.
    \item \textbf{Team capacity.} Single developer; scope must remain MVP-sized with incremental releases.
    \item \textbf{Scope limits.} No payments, advanced analytics, wearables, or outdoor crag support in the MVP.
    \item \textbf{Compliance.} Must satisfy App Store/Play UGC rules and GDPR (consent, minimal PII, deletion/export).
    \item \textbf{Content/IP and venue rules.} Uploaders must own rights; respect filming policies of partner gyms.
\end{itemize}

\section{Modeling}

\subsection{Data Model (ER)}

The data model was designed to support both the climber perspective (ascent logging, feedback, and media) and the gym perspective (route management, seasons/leaderboards, and aggregated analytics). At its core, \textit{users}, \textit{gyms}, and \textit{routes} form the main domain entities. Each route belongs to a gym and stores relevant metadata (grade system/value, color, wall section, setter, and lifecycle status).

Ascent logging is represented by \textit{route\_ascents}, which associates a user to a route and stores information such as whether the route was sent, the date, rating, and optional comments. Style characterization is supported by a controlled taxonomy (\textit{style\_tags}). Users can select tags per ascent using the association table \textit{ascent\_style\_tags}. These raw votes can then be aggregated into \textit{route\_style\_statistics} (per-route) and \textit{gym\_style\_statistics} (per-gym), enabling trend and profile analysis without recomputing everything in real time.

Motivation and community features are supported by \textit{badges}/\textit{user\_badges} and by \textit{leaderboards} with entries and participation snapshots. Optional \textit{prizes} can be linked to leaderboard rankings to encourage consistent participation.

\section{Introduction}

The Beta Breaker application has been implemented as a cross-platform mobile application using React Native \cite{RNDo25} and Expo \cite{ExDo25}, backed entirely by Supabase \cite{SuDo25} for database, authentication, storage, and real-time capabilities. As of February 2026, the project has completed 69 development steps across 13 phases (12 fully completed, Phase~13 in progress), covering all core platform features: authentication, route catalog, session logging, offline support, QR scanning, gamification, social features, notifications, competitions, beta video sharing, and monetization.

The implementation follows a strict Test-Driven Development (TDD) methodology, resulting in 1\,032 unit tests and 408 integration tests (1\,440 total) across 139 test suites.

\section{Architecture}

The system is organized into two main tiers: a React Native \cite{RNDo25} client built with Expo \cite{ExDo25}, and a Supabase \cite{SuDo25} backend that provides the full server-side infrastructure --- PostgreSQL database, authentication (GoTrue), object storage, real-time change streams, and serverless Edge Functions.

This architecture pushes authorization down to the database level: every table is protected by Row Level Security (RLS) \cite{PgDo25} policies that enforce access control based on the authenticated user's identity and role. Because RLS is evaluated by PostgreSQL itself, the security boundary cannot be bypassed by client-side code or misconfigured API routes --- a stronger guarantee than middleware-based authorization in a traditional API server. The client communicates with the database through PostgREST (auto-generated REST endpoints) and with auxiliary services through four Deno-based Edge Functions: QR code signing, push notification dispatch, in-app purchase verification, and billing webhooks.

State management is organized into three layers:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Server state (TanStack Query v5 \cite{TaDo25}):} All data from Supabase (routes, ascents, leaderboards, badges). The database is the source of truth; the client caches and auto-refetches.
    \item \textbf{Client state (Zustand 5.x \cite{ZuDo25}):} Ephemeral UI state such as session timer, pending logs, active filters, and bottom sheet visibility. Minimal boilerplate, no providers required.
    \item \textbf{Local persistence (expo-sqlite):} Offline route cache with 24-hour TTL and an offline action queue. On reconnect, actions are replayed with exponential backoff (1s, 4s, 16s).
\end{enumerate}

The data flow follows four patterns:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Reads:} Screen $\rightarrow$ TanStack Query hook $\rightarrow$ service layer $\rightarrow$ PostgREST $\rightarrow$ Postgres (RLS-filtered).
    \item \textbf{Writes:} User action $\rightarrow$ Zustand optimistic update $\rightarrow$ useMutation $\rightarrow$ service $\rightarrow$ Postgres triggers (streak/badge/leaderboard) $\rightarrow$ cache invalidation.
    \item \textbf{Offline:} Action $\rightarrow$ offlineStore queue (SQLite) $\rightarrow$ [reconnect] $\rightarrow$ drain queue $\rightarrow$ service layer $\rightarrow$ invalidate caches.
    \item \textbf{Realtime:} Supabase Realtime subscription $\rightarrow$ Postgres emits changes $\rightarrow$ TanStack Query cache updated.
\end{itemize}

The codebase is organized into clearly separated layers, each with a single responsibility.

\subsection{Database Layer (18 Migrations)}

The Supabase PostgreSQL database contains 30 tables organized across 18 sequential migrations:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Core tables:} \texttt{profiles}, \texttt{gyms}, \texttt{routes}, \texttt{route\_ascents}, \texttt{style\_tags}, \texttt{route\_style\_tags}, \texttt{user\_gym\_roles}.
    \item \textbf{Social tables:} \texttt{route\_feedback}, \texttt{route\_feedback\_votes}, \texttt{route\_media}, \texttt{content\_reports}, \texttt{follows}.
    \item \textbf{Gamification tables:} \texttt{badges} (19 seeded), \texttt{user\_badges}, \texttt{user\_streaks}, \texttt{leaderboard\_entries}, \texttt{challenges}, \texttt{user\_challenge\_progress}.
    \item \textbf{Competition tables:} \texttt{events}, \texttt{event\_routes}, \texttt{event\_categories}, \texttt{competition\_scores}.
    \item \textbf{System tables:} \texttt{notifications}, \texttt{push\_tokens}, \texttt{notification\_preferences}, \texttt{saved\_routes}, \texttt{audit\_log}, \texttt{maintenance\_tickets}, \texttt{seasons}, \texttt{subscription\_\allowbreak events}.
\end{itemize}

All tables have RLS enabled with 126 policies. Fifteen \texttt{SECURITY DEFINER} functions and five triggers handle automated logic (badge awarding, streak updates, leaderboard computation, profile creation, pinned badge enforcement).

\subsection{Service Layer (18 Services)}

Services are thin wrappers around Supabase PostgREST. They contain no business logic, only query construction:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \texttt{auth.service.ts} --- Sign-up, sign-in, sign-out, password reset, session management.
    \item \texttt{routes.service.ts} --- Route queries with grade, style, and status filters.
    \item \texttt{sessions.service.ts} --- Ascent logging, session summaries, session history.
    \item \texttt{leaderboard.service.ts} --- Three scoring models (hardest grade, flash rate, volume).
    \item \texttt{media.service.ts} --- Beta video upload to Supabase Storage, retrieval, deletion.
    \item \texttt{purchase.service.ts} --- Wraps \texttt{react-native-iap} \cite{IaDo25} for IAP purchase and receipt verification.
    \item Plus 13 additional services for gyms, profiles, feedback, social, events, scores, challenges, badges, notifications, moderation, saved routes, and subscriptions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hook Layer (23 Hooks)}

Each hook wraps a service in TanStack Query, providing caching, loading states, error handling, and cache invalidation:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \texttt{useAuth} --- Authentication state with \texttt{onAuthStateChange} listener and \texttt{refreshProfile()}.
    \item \texttt{useRoutes} --- Route list query with infinite scroll and filter support.
    \item \texttt{useSubscription} --- Purchase and restore mutations with receipt verification.
    \item \texttt{useScoreboardRealtime} --- Live competition scoreboard via Supabase Realtime.
    \item \texttt{useOfflineSync} --- Offline queue drain with exponential backoff on reconnect.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Store Layer (3 Zustand Stores)}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \texttt{sessionStore} --- Active climbing session state (timer, pending logs, duration).
    \item \texttt{offlineStore} --- Offline action queue backed by expo-sqlite.
    \item \texttt{routeFilterStore} --- Active route filter selections (grade, style, wall).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Utility Layer (14 Pure Functions)}

Pure TypeScript functions with no side effects, covering: grade conversion (V-scale, Font, YDS with canonical integer mapping 0--30), streak calculation, leaderboard scoring, competition scoring, challenge criteria evaluation, Zod validation schemas, time formatting, ISO week utilities, geolocation, QR code parsing, video validation, entitlement checks, and CSV/PDF export.

\subsection{Edge Functions (4 Deno Functions)}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \texttt{sign-qr} --- Signs QR code payloads with EC P-256 keys; admin-authenticated.
    \item \texttt{dispatch-push} --- Sends push notifications via Expo Push API with preference checks.
    \item \texttt{verify-iap} --- Validates App Store / Play Store receipts, inserts subscription events, updates user tier.
    \item \texttt{billing-webhook} --- Handles store renewal, cancellation, and expiration notifications.
\end{itemize}

Components are organized by domain: \texttt{ui/} (10 primitives: Button, Card, Badge, BadgeIcon, Avatar, TextInput, IconButton, Divider, StarRating, ExternalLink), \texttt{routes/} (RouteCard, FilterBar, VideoUploadButton, OwnershipModal, BetaVideoPlayer), \texttt{session/} (QuickLogSheet, SessionTimer, SessionSummary), \texttt{social/} (FeedItem, FeedbackComposer, FollowButton, ReportSheet), \texttt{badges/} (BadgePicker, ProfileBadges), \texttt{streaks/} (StreakCard, StreakStatusBanner), \texttt{challenges/} (ChallengeCard, ChallengeProgress), \texttt{notifications/} (NotificationBell, NotificationItem), \texttt{navigation/} (CustomTabBar with FAB), and \texttt{subscription/} (Paywall).

\section{Test Approach}

The project follows a strict Test-Driven Development (TDD) methodology where every implementation step begins with failing tests (Red), followed by the minimum code to pass (Green), and then refactoring. This approach ensures that every feature has test coverage from inception and that regressions are caught immediately.

\section{Test Pyramid}

Testing is organized into three levels:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Unit tests (1\,032 tests):} Pure utility functions, service query construction, hook behavior, and component rendering. Services mock the Supabase client; hooks mock the service layer; components mock hooks.

    \item \textbf{Integration tests (408 tests):} Run against a real local PostgreSQL instance via \texttt{supabase start}. These tests verify RLS policies, database triggers, stored functions, CASCADE behavior, and UNIQUE constraints. Each test authenticates as a specific user to confirm that row-level security correctly filters data.

    \item \textbf{End-to-end tests (Phase 20):} Planned for a later phase using Maestro or Detox for full user-flow validation on device.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Tools and Infrastructure}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Jest} \cite{JeDo25} with \texttt{jest-expo} preset --- Test runner with TypeScript support via \texttt{ts-jest}.
    \item \textbf{React Native Testing Library} --- Behavior-driven component testing (render, press, assert text).
    \item \textbf{Local Supabase (Docker)} --- Real Postgres with Auth and RLS for integration tests.
    \item \textbf{GitHub Actions CI} --- Automated lint $\rightarrow$ type-check $\rightarrow$ test on every pull request.
\end{itemize}

\section{Risk Analysis}

The main testing risks and their mitigations are:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Native module mocking:} Libraries like \texttt{react-native-iap}, \texttt{expo-camera}, and \texttt{expo-video} cannot run in a Jest environment. \emph{Mitigation:} comprehensive \texttt{jest.mock()} factories in each test file that simulate the native API surface.
    \item \textbf{RLS policy correctness:} Incorrect policies could leak data across users. \emph{Mitigation:} 408 integration tests run against real Postgres, authenticating as different users and verifying that SELECT/INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE are properly scoped.
    \item \textbf{Offline sync conflicts:} Queued actions may conflict with server state. \emph{Mitigation:} last-write-wins strategy with exponential backoff and unit tests covering queue drain, retry, and failure scenarios.
\end{itemize}

\section{Development Methodology}

The project follows an incremental, test-driven approach organized into 21 sequential phases. Each phase is broken into numbered steps, and each step follows the TDD cycle:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Red:} Write failing tests that define the expected behavior.
    \item \textbf{Green:} Write the minimum code to make the tests pass.
    \item \textbf{Refactor:} Clean up duplication, improve naming, re-run all tests.
\end{enumerate}

Version control follows a trunk-based workflow on the \texttt{main} branch, with atomic commits per step. Each commit message references the step number (e.g., ``feat: add QR scanner screen (Step 7.1)''). A GitHub Actions CI pipeline runs lint, type-check, and the full test suite on every push.

\section{Conclusion}

This report documents the specification and implementation of Beta Breaker, a mobile platform for indoor climbing gyms. The project addressed the identified problem --- fragmented route knowledge and lack of structured gym-climber feedback --- by building a comprehensive system that connects climbers and gyms through route discovery, ascent logging, beta sharing, and gamification.

The implementation followed a strict TDD methodology across 13 phases and 69 steps, producing 1\,440 automated tests (1\,032 unit + 408 integration). The system covers all core features required for an MVP: authentication, route catalog, session logging, offline support, QR scanning, gamification (19 badges, streaks, challenges), social features (leaderboards, follows, feedback, moderation), push notifications, competitions with live scoreboards, beta video sharing, and Pro subscription monetization via in-app purchases.

The architecture --- Supabase-first with no custom backend, TanStack Query for server state, Zustand for client state, and expo-sqlite for offline persistence --- proved effective for a single-developer project, minimizing infrastructure complexity while maintaining security through database-level RLS policies.

\section{Future Work}

The remaining work includes the completion of Phase~13 (trials, promo codes, gym billing) and Phases~14--21:

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Phase 13 (remaining) --- Monetization:} Trial activation, promo code redemption, and gym billing console (\texteuro\,0.50/active user/month).
    \item \textbf{Phase 14 --- Progression \& Analytics:} Grade pyramid visualization, style insights radar chart, and personalized route suggestions (Pro-gated).
    \item \textbf{Phase 15 --- Admin Web Portal:} Gym dashboard with route management, bulk operations, and audit log.
    \item \textbf{Phase 16 --- Profile \& Onboarding:} Profile editing, onboarding flow, and gym selection.
    \item \textbf{Phase 17 --- Score Entry \& Verification:} QR-based athlete score entry, judge workflow, and verification flow for competitions.
    \item \textbf{Phase 18 --- Internationalization:} Language switching (EN, PT-PT) and accessibility modes (large text, color-aware holds).
    \item \textbf{Phase 19 --- Polish \& Monitoring:} Sentry error tracking, performance monitoring, and UI refinements.
    \item \textbf{Phase 20 --- End-to-End Testing:} Maestro or Detox tests for critical user flows.
    \item \textbf{Phase 21 --- Deployment:} Production EAS builds, store submission, and OTA update pipeline.
\end{enumerate}

Completing these phases will result in a production-ready platform deployable to the App Store and Google Play, ready for pilot testing with partner climbing gyms.
